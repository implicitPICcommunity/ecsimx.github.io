Numerical foundations

The ECsim algorithm

An energy-conserving, semi-implicit Particle-in-Cell method that stays stable at large time steps and coarse resolution, with a single linear field solve per step.

Particle-in-Cell fundamentals

A Particle-in-Cell (PIC) model represents a plasma with computational particles that move in continuous space while the fields live on a grid. Each cycle gathers particle moments (charge, current) onto the grid, solves Maxwell’s equations, and interpolates the fields back to push the particles.

dxp/dt = vp,   mp dvp/dt = qp(E + vp × B)

Explicit PIC codes need the time step and cell size to resolve the fastest plasma scales (plasma frequency and Debye length) to stay stable. Implicit moment and semi-implicit schemes relax these limits by coupling particles and fields in the same step.

The ECsim idea

ECsim computes the current produced by each particle during a time step analytically, as a linear response to the electric field. That response is collected in a mass matrix and folded into Maxwell’s equations, so the field solve is a single linear system. Particle and field energies are then exchanged consistently, without nonlinear iteration. According to the ECsim papers, the method:

  • is unconditionally stable in time and eliminates the finite-grid instability;
  • follows the same cycle structure as an explicit PIC code;
  • has a cost comparable to other semi-implicit PIC codes;
  • conserves energy exactly for θ = ½, where it is also second-order accurate.

The time cycle

1 · Moment gathering

For every species s and node N, the implicit current Ĵ and the mass matrix M are accumulated from the particles, using the interpolation weights WpN and the node volume VN:

ĴN = (1/VN) Σs Σp qp v̂p WpN,   v̂p = αp vpn

MNN′ = Σs (βs/VN) Σp qp αp WpN′ WpN

with βs = qsΔt/(2ms) and the rotation tensor, evaluated with the magnetic field interpolated to the particle:

αp = [1 / (1 + β²|Bp|²)] ( I − β I×Bp + β² BpBp )

Notation follows Gonzalez-Herrero et al.; consult the paper for the exact index conventions.

2 · Field solve

Ĵ and M enter Maxwell’s equations, discretized with a θ-scheme, giving one coupled linear system for En+θ and Bn+θ. The system has 3(NxNyNz + CxCyCz) unknowns for N nodes and C cells per direction, and is solved matrix-free with GMRES. The fields at the end of the step follow by extrapolation:

En+1 = (1/θ) En+θ − ((1−θ)/θ) En

3 · Particle mover

With En+θ known, each particle is advanced explicitly, with no iteration:

v̄p = αp( vpn + βsEpn+θ ),   vpn+1 = 2v̄p − vpn,   xpn+3/2 = xpn+1/2 + Δt vpn+1

The mass matrix

Each node couples to its 3×3×3 neighbourhood, i.e. 27 possible entries, each a 3×3 tensor. Because M is symmetric (MNN′ = MN′N), only 14 neighbour entries per node need to be stored. Building M is the most expensive part of an ECsim step: in the published CPU profile (512 MPI processes, 160³ cells) moment gathering took almost 90% of runtime, and about 75% of that was mass-matrix computation. This is why moment gathering is the first target for GPU acceleration.

Energy conservation

The exchange of energy between particles and fields is balanced exactly at the discrete level when θ = ½, the setting that also gives second-order accuracy. The choice of θ does not affect stability. Values θ > ½ add numerical damping of high-frequency waves; the whistler-wave study in the ECsim paper used θ = 1 for that purpose, which trades away exact energy conservation.

What “exact” means in practice. Conservation holds for the discrete equations. In a run, energy is conserved to within the accuracy of the linear solver (GMRES tolerance) and floating-point round-off. The derivation is in the original ECsim formulation (Lapenta, JCP 2017); the website does not reproduce it.

Stability and accuracy evidence

The ECsim paper compares ECsim with iPic3D, another semi-implicit PIC code, on a 2D whistler-wave problem (box 20 di × 1.25 di, 50 particles per cell, ion-to-electron mass ratio 100).

  • In a spatial-resolution scan from 1024 down to 128 cells, iPic3D’s total energy grew by about 3% over 1600 cycles at 128 cells, and its smoothed k–ω spectra were distorted.
  • The price: for the 1024-cell case, ECsim needed about 3.2× the runtime of iPic3D. The authors argue this is acceptable because ECsim can use a time step about ten times larger and a spatial resolution about four times coarser.

Trade-offs and limitations

  • Cost per step: mass-matrix assembly is much more expensive than moment gathering in other semi-implicit codes.
  • Particle count: the paper notes that at least about 100 particles per cell are typically needed to control noise; ECsim does not use spatial smoothing.
  • Test scope: the whistler comparison is 2D and against one reference code; the performance runs cover 10 cycles.

Simulation workflow

Initialize

Set species, grid, time step, θ, particles per cell, and initial fields.

Gather moments

Accumulate Ĵ and the mass matrices on the grid (offloaded to the GPU in ECsim-X).

Solve the fields

Solve the linear Maxwell system for En+θ and Bn+θ with GMRES.

Move particles

Update velocities and positions explicitly (offloaded to the GPU in ECsim-X).

Diagnose and repeat

Write output and track kinetic, field, and total energy.
