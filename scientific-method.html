Numerical foundations

The science behind ECsim-X

Particle-in-Cell methods couple kinetic particle dynamics to electromagnetic fields on a computational grid. ECsim-X is described as implementing an Energy-Conserving Semi-Implicit method; details below provide a conceptual introduction and should be reconciled with the project’s published method and source code.

Particle-in-Cell fundamentals

In a Particle-in-Cell (PIC) model, charged particles represent the kinetic distribution of a plasma. Particle positions and velocities are advanced in time, while charge and current moments are mapped to a spatial mesh. Electromagnetic fields are then computed on the grid and interpolated back to particle locations.

dxp/dt = vp,   mp dvp/dt = qp(E + vp × B)

These equations express the Lorentz-force motion of a particle. The precise time centering, field discretization, and particle-grid shape functions depend on the implementation.

Energy-conserving semi-implicit methods

Semi-implicit schemes treat selected couplings implicitly while retaining an explicit or predictor-based structure for other operations. The ECsim method is designed around energy-conserving particle-field coupling. Exact algorithmic steps, assumptions, and conservation properties should be documented from the canonical ECsim-X references and implementation.

Particle-grid coupling

Two central operations connect particle and field representations:

  • Deposition: particle properties are accumulated on grid locations to construct charge or current quantities.
  • Interpolation: grid-based electric and magnetic fields are evaluated at particle positions.

Consistent discretization of these operations is important for numerical behavior, parallel correctness, and energy accounting.

Energy and validation

A useful validation workflow tracks particle kinetic energy, field energy, and any energy exchange or source terms included in the model. Diagnostics should define units, normalization, boundary contributions, and whether reported conservation is exact, bounded, or approximate.

Wtotal = Wparticles + Wfields + Wsources / boundaries

This is a bookkeeping template rather than a claim about the exact diagnostic implemented in ECsim-X.

Typical PIC workflow

Initialize

Set the physical parameters, mesh, particle distribution, and initial fields.

Deposit particle quantities

Map the required particle moments to the grid.

Update electromagnetic fields

Apply the selected field discretization and semi-implicit coupling.

Advance particles

Interpolate fields and update particle states according to the numerical scheme.

Diagnose and repeat

Record physical diagnostics and numerical invariants before advancing to the next step.
